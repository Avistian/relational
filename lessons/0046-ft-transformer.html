FT-Transformer (feature tokenizer + [CLS] readout)

Lesson 046 · Phase 2 · Q1 · primary reading: Gorishniy, Rubachev, Khrulkov & Babenko 2021, Revisiting Deep Learning Models for Tabular Data

L045 ended on a specific complaint. TabTransformer contextualises the categorical columns beautifully — but the numeric features never enter the attention: they are LayerNorm'd and glued on after the Transformer. On a numeric-heavy table most of the signal lives in features the attention never touches, which is why its contextual edge was so thin and why it lost to a cheap tree. This lesson is the fix. FT-Transformer (Gorishniy et al. 2021 — the same paper that gave us the strong MLP/ResNet baselines) makes every feature a token, numerics included, and reads the whole row out through a single [CLS] token.

Two claims to keep separate, exactly as in L043–L045. First, the within-family claim: does tokenising the numerics make FT-Transformer beat TabTransformer, and is the gap biggest where numerics carry the signal? Second, the paper's headline: is FT-Transformer the strongest single neural baseline — better than a tuned MLP — while still only tying gradient-boosted trees under a fair protocol?

Its single skill: build the FT-Transformer feature tokenizer (a per-feature linear embedding for every numeric feature, an embedding table for every categorical feature) plus a [CLS] readout, train it, and show it is the best neural model of the four we have built — beating TabTransformer most where numerics dominate — yet still losing the flat-table metric to CatBoost.

One sentence. Trained from scratch, FT-Transformer takes mean rank 2.50 across four tables — ahead of the MLP (2.75) and well ahead of TabTransformer (3.75), so it is the strongest single neural baseline — and it beats TabTransformer on 3 of 4 tables, losing only on the most categorical one; but CatBoost still wins all four (rank 1.00), so the honest verdict is unchanged: the best neural tabular model of Year 2 so far, still a notch below a tree on flat data.

Recap: where TabTransformer stops

Here is the L045 architecture again, because FT-Transformer is a targeted edit to it. Walk to stage 3 — the continuous bypass — and notice what it means: the Transformer runs over the categorical tokens only, and the numerics are merely normalised and concatenated before the head.

TabTransformer (Huang 2020), the model you trained in L045. Stage 3 is the ceiling: numeric features skip the attention entirely. FT-Transformer removes stages "embed categoricals only" and "bypass" and replaces them with a single tokenizer that treats every feature the same way.

The one idea: tokenize every feature

A Transformer operates on a sequence of tokens — vectors of a common width d. TabTransformer only knew how to make tokens out of categories (an embedding lookup). FT-Transformer's Feature Tokenizer (Gorishniy 2021, §3.3, Fig. 2) adds the missing half: a way to turn a number into a token too.

Feature Tokenizer. Every feature becomes one d-dimensional token.
  • Numeric feature j: T_j = b_j + x_j · W_j, where W_j and b_j are learned d-vectors. The token is an affine map of the scalar x_j — the same feature at value 39 and value 40 lands at nearby points along the learned direction W_j.
  • Categorical feature j: T_j = b_j + e_j[x_j] — the entity embedding of L031 plus a per-feature bias.
Stack the k feature tokens into a [k, d] sequence and you have turned a heterogeneous row of numbers-and-strings into the homogeneous token sequence a Transformer expects.
numeric T_j = b_j + x_j · W_j (W_j, b_j ∈ ℝ^d — one learned vector per feature) categorical T_j = b_j + e_j[x_j] (embedding lookup + per-feature bias)

The other borrowed idea is the [CLS] token from BERT. Prepend one extra learned vector at position 0 that represents no feature. Its only job is to collect, through attention, whatever the row's feature tokens contain — and after the Transformer stack, that single [CLS] vector is the row's summary that the head reads. Walk the flow:

FT-Transformer's data-flow (Gorishniy 2021, Fig. 2). (1) every feature — numeric and categorical — becomes a token; (2) a learned [CLS] token is prepended; (3) N Transformer layers let all k+1 tokens attend to one another; (4) the final [CLS] vector is read out by a small head (LayerNorm → ReLU → Linear). The one change from TabTransformer is Stage 1 — numerics are now first-class tokens, so a change in age can reshape the whole row's summary.

Why "numerics attend" is the whole point. In TabTransformer, changing a numeric feature could never change the contextual embeddings — those come only from categorical tokens (we verified the move is exactly 0.0). In FT-Transformer, a numeric feature is a token in the same sequence as everything else, so it both attends and is attended to: bumping one numeric input moves the [CLS] readout by L2 0.438 in our check. That single structural change is the difference between the two models — and, as the bake-off shows, it matters most exactly where numerics carry the signal.

Training it: the same fair protocol

Nothing about training is FT-Transformer-specific — the point of Gorishniy 2021 is that architectures should be compared under one protocol. We reuse the shared recipe of every neural arm since L042: mini-batch AdamW, early stopping on validation ROC-AUC, identical split / metric / selection rule for every model. Our from-scratch relkit.ft_transformer implements the tokenizer, the [CLS] token, PreNorm Transformer blocks (reusing the multi-head attention validated to machine precision in L045), and the [CLS] readout head. The attention kernel is checked against torch's own, not imported.

Does tokenising numerics help? FT-Transformer vs the field

The cleanest experiment races the four neural-and-tree models we have built — FT-Transformer, TabTransformer (the numeric-bypass predecessor), a tuned MLP (the strong deep baseline), and CatBoost (the honest tree bar) — on four tables spanning the numeric/categorical spectrum, from credit_g (mostly categorical) to phoneme (all numeric). Read this the disciplined way. Three readings, kept apart. (1) The numeric-bypass fix works: FT-Transformer beats TabTransformer on every table except credit_g — the most categorical one (num-frac 0.35), where the numeric tokenizer has least to add and the two tie (0.785 vs 0.787, within noise). On adult, churn, and phoneme — where numerics carry more of the signal — the gap is large (up to +0.046 on phoneme). That is the mechanism, made visible. (2) FT-T is the strongest single neural baseline: it out-ranks the tuned MLP, matching the paper's central finding. (3) It still loses to trees: CatBoost wins all four, cheaper and faster. Gorishniy 2021's honest headline is exactly this — FT-Transformer is the best deep model and only ties GBDTs on average; on some tables (Adult included) the tree still edges it. Our small run reproduces that shape precisely.

Reproduce the paper's result

The bake-off above is a down-scaled demonstration, not Gorishniy 2021's Table 2 (11 datasets, tuned with Optuna, fixed splits). To actually train FT-Transformer like in the paper, the lab ships a paper-results harness (labs/_paper_repro_l046.py) with three budgets — smoke (seconds), closer (a T4, tens of minutes, d=64), and paper (hours, the paper's d≈192) — runnable on a Colab GPU or unattended via modal run --detach modal/l046_paper_repro.py --preset closer. It re-runs the same from-scratch model on a paper dataset (Adult, or Higgs-small) against TabTransformer, an MLP, and CatBoost, and prints a conclusion ledger. Because we use a random OpenML split and no Optuna tuning, the absolute Table 2 accuracy stays INCOMPARABLE; what you read are the DIRECTION tests — FT-T > TabTransformer, FT-T ≥ MLP, and FT-T ≈ CatBoost — which is what actually updates belief. Until you run it, the paper's tuned number stays cited, not reproduced.

Thesis bridge

What today buys the thesis. Two things. Methodologically, FT-Transformer is the honest ceiling of single-table deep learning: the strongest of the four neural models we built, and it still only ties trees on flat data — so any claim that "deep learning wins on tabular" has to clear this bar, not a weak MLP strawman. That is the baseline the whole relational thesis must eventually beat. Architecturally, the feature tokenizer + [CLS] is the row-encoder blueprint the rest of the curriculum reuses: turn a heterogeneous entity into a set of tokens, let them attend, and pool a summary. In a relational database an entity's tokens are not just its own columns but the columns of related rows reached across foreign keys — and the [CLS] readout becomes the node representation a graph model passes along an edge. FT-Transformer is the within-row rehearsal of the across-table encoder the thesis is built on; you will meet it again as an RDL row-encoder ancestor.
