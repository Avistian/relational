Year 1 Exit Exam

Lesson 040 · Phase 1 · Q4 · ~60 min · capstone · primary reading: Grinsztajn et al. 2022 (exit re-read) + your L039 essay

Forty lessons ago the mission asked you to prove a contrarian bet: that relational deep learning is undervalued because the single-table paradigm misses structure a flatten destroys. Year 1 did not ask you to prove the bet. It asked you to build the bar the bet must clear — a leak-free, tuned tree ensemble on a flat table — and to understand why that bar is stubborn. This lesson is the gate. The curriculum names it plainly: beat XGBoost on a flat task, or explain why not.

Either fork is a pass. A real beat under a fair protocol is rare on typical flat tables — Year 1 spent a quarter measuring that. An honest match, followed by a written explanation that cites Grinsztajn's three inductive biases and stands on (or revises) your L039 synthesis, is the modal Year-1 exit. What fails the exit is a soft-sold "win," a non-regenerable number, or an explanation that says "trees are more powerful" without mechanisms.

Its single skill: under a fair fixed protocol on a flat Tier-A task, regenerate a disclosed XGBoost baseline, attempt one honest Year-1 challenger, classify the outcome as BEAT / TIE / EXPLAIN, and — whether you beat or not — write Grinsztajn's three inductive biases with evidence of record, ending in a regenerable number and an explicit STAND or REVISE stance on the L039 synthesis claim.

One sentence. The Year 1 exit is passed by a regenerable XGBoost number plus a written inductive-bias understanding — and "I matched the bar within noise, here is why further single-table cleverness did not clear it" is a full pass, not a consolation prize.

The exit contract — six gates

Before any of BEAT / TIE / EXPLAIN is legitimate, six gates must close. Click each gate. The default is the regenerable baseline — the first concrete deliverable the curriculum names ("reproducible tuned tree baseline").

Exit criterion (curriculum). Reproducible tuned tree baseline + written understanding of Grinsztajn's three inductive biases. The lab title ("beat XGB or explain why not") is the experimental fork that produces those two deliverables — not a demand that you must win.
Noise band (this exit). On adult under the L020 protocol, a gap of ±0.002 ROC-AUC is treated as a match (the same tolerance L020's CHECK cells used). Essay stance. After the number, one explicit word: STAND (the exit agrees with your L039 working claim) or REVISE (name what changed). Revising because you found a real beat, a broken boundary, or a protocol defect is scientific. Revising to claim an RDL fair-bar win Year 1 has not shown is not.

Written criterion — three biases, cold

The curriculum's second exit deliverable is written understanding, not a quiz score. Restate each bias from first principles (standard #17), then use the widget to preview the geometry. Your lab EXIT ticket will ask for one sentence per bias with a verified number and a flip condition.

1 · Irregular (non-smooth) targets. Gradient descent on neural nets has a spectral / smoothness bias: it fits smooth functions easily and irregular ones poorly. Tabular targets are often jagged — thresholds, fees, hard cuts. Trees place axis-aligned steps; nets round them off. Flip: Gaussian-smooth the target and the GBT–MLP gap collapses toward zero (L025).
2 · Privileged column orientation. A tree's splits attend to each meaningful column in the original basis. An MLP is rotationally invariant (W·(Qx) = (WQ)·x) — a feature when pixels are arbitrary orientations, a liability when columns carry individual meaning. Flip: a lossless random rotation collapses the tree (0.987→0.747) and leaves the MLP unmoved (L026 / Ng 2004).
3 · Junk-feature robustness. Trees gate uninformative columns via greedy, gain-gated splits (implicit selection). MLPs feed every column into layer 1 and, being rotation-invariant, need at least linearly more samples per junk feature. Flip: on a smooth target where the MLP wins clean, adding pure-noise columns reverses the ranking (L027).

Thesis bridge

Why this exit is the mission's Year-1 hinge. The skeptic says: "Just beat XGBoost on a table — why invent RDL?" Year 1's honest answer is often: on a typical flat table, under a fair protocol, I matched the bar, and here is the inductive-bias reason further single-table cleverness did not clear it — while the unpaid signal may already have been destroyed by the join. That is not a failed exit. That is the setup for Years 2–6. A Year-1 beat of XGB, if you get one, must still survive the peer-review checklist — and still leaves the relational open burden open.
