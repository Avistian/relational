# Review — Empirical Evaluation of Out-Of-Distribution Performance of Tabular Foundation Models

- **arXiv:** [2607.26000](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26000) (v1, cs.LG, Jul 2026)
- **Authors:** Malena Loza, David Chushig-Muzo, Eva Milara, Luis Bote-Curiel, Luis Estrada-Petrocelli, Felipe Grijalva (USFQ Quito; URJC Madrid; Univ. Latina de Panamá)

## Problem

TFMs are trained and benchmarked almost entirely under the IID assumption, but
real deployments face distribution shift. The robustness of TFMs to shift is
underexplored. The paper delivers **the first systematic OOD evaluation of modern
TFMs**, asking whether pre-training strategy (synthetic SCM prior vs. real-data
vs. mixed) confers any inherent robustness, and whether the ID↔OOD relationship
documented for classical tabular models extends to TFMs.

## Method

- **Nine TFMs** spanning three pre-training corpora: real-world
  (TabPFN v2/v2.5/v2.6/v3, TabFM), synthetic SCM prior (TabICL, TabICLv2, LimiX),
  and mixed SCM+tree-ensemble prior (Mitra).
- **Three TableShift datasets**, each a different shift type in a different
  domain: **HELOC** (label shift, finance), **Childhood Lead** (socioeconomic
  subpopulation shift by poverty level, public health), **Voting**
  (geographic/region shift, ~380 features). Each has train / ID-test / OOD-test
  splits.
- **Protocol.** Default inference config, no fine-tuning, no hyperparameter
  tuning; TableShift training split used as ICL context. Metric = ROC-AUC;
  **shift gap Δ = ID − OOD**. 5 seeds, mean±std. TabFM/LimiX omitted on Voting due
  to memory cost.

## Key results

- **Universal degradation.** Every model on every dataset degrades OOD (all points
  below the no-degradation diagonal); shift gaps range ~0.003–0.060 by shift type.
- **Real-data pre-training buys ID performance, not robustness.** TabPFN variants
  top both ID and OOD absolute scores, but their **shift gaps are comparable to
  synthetic-prior models** — the OOD advantage is driven by stronger ID fitting,
  not superior generalization. Group means: Real OOD ≈ 0.79 (widest IQR) but
  largest mean gap ≈ 0.019; Mixed ≈ 0.015; **Synthetic lowest gap ≈ 0.012**.
- **ID↔OOD relationship extends to TFMs** (Gardner et al. finding): higher ID ⇒
  proportionally higher OOD, with no model breaking above/below the degradation
  line. Improvements from bigger context / architecture are reflected
  proportionally under shift but do not add inherent robustness.
- **Shift type dominates.** Ordering of degradation: **Childhood Lead
  (socioeconomic) > HELOC (label) > Voting (geographic)** — consistent across all
  pre-training strategies, so the degradation mechanism is prior-independent.
- **Mitra is label-shift-sensitive.** Competitive under covariate/subpopulation
  and geographic shift but the **largest OOD drop on HELOC** — its SCM+tree prior
  handles covariate shift but not label-prevalence change (caution flag for
  clinical risk deployment).
- **Scalability gap.** The highest-performing TFMs are the least scalable. LimiX
  cross-attention scales O(n_train·n_test·d_model), needing chunking even on a
  47 GB GPU; TabFM needed OOM-avoiding chunking; Mitra hard-caps at 10k training
  rows. Standard benchmarks hide this by using small datasets.

## Fit with the tabular curriculum

A fresh **multi-model OOD baseline** that sharpens two threads at once: the
**Y2 Q3 "where TabPFN v2 breaks"** critical lectures (distribution shift as a
first-class failure mode, with label shift the most damaging) and the
**BeyondArena / non-IID honest-evaluation** thread. The prior-type analysis is a
useful, if coarse, empirical counterpoint to "what the synthetic SCM prior buys
you" — it argues real vs. synthetic priors differ in ID fit but *not* in
robustness. The scalability-gap finding also feeds the deployment-realism thread
(leaderboard wins ≠ deployable).

## Caveats / limitations

- **Only 3 datasets / 3 shift types** and one metric (ROC-AUC); shift-gap
  magnitudes (0.003–0.060) are small and dataset-idiosyncratic — thin evidence
  for strong general claims.
- **Default configs only, no adaptation** — says nothing about whether
  fine-tuning/calibration could close OOD gaps.
- **Incomplete grid:** TabFM and LimiX missing on Voting (compute), weakening the
  cross-model comparison exactly where features are highest-dimensional.
- **No classical/tree baselines re-run here** — the "extends to TFMs" claim leans
  on the original TableShift numbers rather than a head-to-head in this study.
- **Descriptive, not mechanistic:** identifies that TFMs degrade but not why, or
  how prior design would change it.

## Take-home

Across nine TFMs and three shift types, **no TFM has inherent OOD robustness**:
all degrade systematically, the ID↔OOD proportional-degradation law from
classical tabular ML carries over, real-data pre-training raises absolute OOD
scores only via better ID fitting, label/socioeconomic shift hurt most, and the
strongest models are often undeployable at scale — a clean, if small-N, addition
to the "where TFMs break under shift" story.
