# Review — Structural Adversarial Attacks on Relational Deep Learning under Integrity Constraints

- **arXiv:** [2607.07089](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07089) (v1, cs.LG, Jul 2026)
- **Authors:** Alan Gany (Univ. Grenoble Alpes, CNRS, Grenoble INP, LIG), Bogdan Cautis (Singapore Institute of Technology), Silviu Maniu (Univ. Grenoble Alpes)
- **Venue:** VLDB 2026 Workshop on Applied AI for Database Systems and Applications (AIDB)
- **Code:** https://github.com/alanganyDB/Structural-Adversarial-Attacks-on-Relational-Deep-Learning-under-Integrity-Constraints

## Problem

Relational Deep Learning (RDL) is now the standard recipe for ML on relational
databases: encode the DB as a heterogeneous temporal graph (tuples → nodes,
PK–FK dependencies → typed edges) and train a GNN end-to-end. By turning the
database into the model's input, RDL also turns it into an *attack surface* —
but adversarial robustness of this pipeline was previously unstudied. The paper
gives the first such study under a white-box threat model where the attacker
knows the graph construction and training, yet can only act on the *upstream
database*: rewiring foreign-key references while preserving schema integrity
constraints (FK validity, the degree-one FK constraint, and functional
dependencies).

## Method

- **Threat model / edit space.** No tuples inserted or deleted; the only
  admissible perturbation reassigns an FK to another *valid* parent PK (and
  updates the symmetric reverse edge), preserving degree-one referential
  integrity. Dependencies are typed **mutable** (local vs. *coupled* rewiring,
  where jointly-constrained FKs must be edited atomically) or **immutable**
  (entity PKs, sensitive structural FKs) and excluded. Attacks run under a global
  edit budget *B*. The admissible set is combinatorial (∝ C-choose-B) and
  non-additive because GNN message passing couples edits — hence heuristics.
- **Attackable model.** Freeze the pretrained heterogeneous GraphSAGE (2 layers,
  shallow to preserve gradient signal / limit oversmoothing); introduce
  differentiable edge-mask weights so gradients flow through candidate rewirings
  (existing edges weight 1, candidates weight 0).
- **Seven strategies.** Two random baselines (Random, Random+Exact rerank) and
  five gradient-guided variants that score candidates by combined forward+reverse
  gradient sensitivity, with per-relation normalizations (raw, Z-score, robust
  Z-score/MAD, min-max) plus a Gradient+Exact shortlist reranker (FGSM-type
  ℓ∞ direction, derived in the appendix). Evaluated on RelBench **rel-f1** (9
  tables, ~74k rows) across 2 regression + 2 binary-classification tasks, 5 seeds.

## Key results

- **Regression is exploitable; gradients clearly beat random.** On
  `driver-position` MAE rises 3.155 → 3.179 (B=1→100) for the pure gradient
  attack while random stays flat ~3.144; `qualifying-position` 0.995 → 1.013 vs.
  flat 0.994. For a better-fitted model the attack induces up to ~7% relative
  degradation.
- **Classification is much more robust.** Even the strongest attack drops
  `driver-dnf` accuracy only 77.37 → 75.51; rankings are noisy. Attributed to a
  **low label-flip rate** — decision boundaries are locally stable, so
  integrity-preserving rewirings rarely change the predicted class.
- **Gradient-only is the best cost/effect trade-off.** Gradient attacks (~46–54s)
  achieve most of the gain of exact-shortlist reranking (~87–92s) at ~half the
  runtime; random is fast (~0.1s) but ineffective.
- **Normalization changes spread, not strength.** Raw gradient concentrates on
  ~1.8 relations yet stays competitive; normalized variants spread across 5.2–7
  relations. All variants already touch ~486–499 distinct targets at B=500, so
  none produces suspicious hub-like patterns.

## Fit with the tabular curriculum

Feeds the **Y4 error-analysis / robustness thread** directly on RelBench: it
exposes a concrete RDL failure mode — the brittleness of the PK–FK
heterogeneous-temporal-graph + GNN encoding — and does so with a principled,
constraint-respecting threat model rather than arbitrary adjacency edits. Sits
naturally on the **tabular↔relational seam**: the vulnerability is *structural*
(the schema-induced topology), not feature-level, which is exactly the extra
attack surface that relational encoding introduces over single-table tabular
models. The regression-vs-classification asymmetry is a citable, teachable
observation about where GNN-on-DB predictions are and aren't sensitive.

## Caveats / limitations

- **Single dataset (rel-f1) only.** Chosen for its coupled/hierarchical schema,
  but generality across RelBench schemas is explicitly future work.
- **Structure-only, untargeted, white-box.** No feature attacks (text/numeric
  attributes), no targeted "force a specific prediction" attacks, and full
  knowledge of construction + training — a strong attacker assumption. Effects
  are also measured on a *frozen* model over a fixed mini-batch (evasion at
  inference, not poisoning of training).
- **Small absolute effects.** ~7% MAE degradation at best and a couple of
  accuracy points; whether this is "dangerous" depends on the application. Some
  classification rows are non-monotonic in budget (non-additivity of edits).
- **CPU-only heterogeneous GraphSAGE, shallow (2-layer).** Authors note attack
  efficacy is itself sensitive to training config (depth/width/epochs), so
  results may not transfer to deeper or better-tuned RDL models.
- **Descriptive, not defensive.** No robustification is proposed; the authors
  frame the "inverse problem" (robust-by-design RDL) as the more interesting
  follow-up.

## Take-home

The relational structure itself is an exploitable attack surface once a DB is
consumed by a learned model: gradient-guided, integrity-preserving FK rewiring
reliably degrades RDL **regression** on RelBench at low cost, while
**classification** stays robust thanks to low label-flip rates. A first,
single-dataset robustness probe — valuable as a failure-mode exhibit, light on
defenses and breadth.
