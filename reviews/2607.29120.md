# Review — Curriculum Matters: Data-Efficient Relational PFN Pretraining with Synthetic Data

- **arXiv:** [2607.29120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29120) (v1, cs.LG / cs.DB, Jul 2026)
- **Authors:** Mohammad Sadeq Abolhasani, Viswanath Ganapathy
- **Tag:** [RDL]

## Problem

Relational Prior-Data Fitted Networks (PFNs) such as RDB-PFN approximate
Bayesian inference over multi-table databases by pretraining on *millions* of
synthetic tasks. That raw-scale recipe is expensive and opaque. The paper asks
three intertwined questions: (i) can a structurally *different* synthetic
generator — PluRel — substitute for RDB-PFN's own prior? (ii) how much does the
*order* in which synthetic data is presented matter? and (iii) how much
relational reasoning can a PFN acquire from *single-table* synthetic pretraining
alone, before any relational data is seen?

## Method

- **Single synthetic source.** PluRel is the sole data generator across all
  experiments, decoupling the "which generator" question from "how much / in
  what order."
- **Progressive single-table curriculum.** Gradually widen schema complexity
  from 7 → 17 columns, versus an all-at-once control on the same data.
- **Relational curriculum from scratch.** Train directly on PluRel databases and
  evaluate on the 19-task RelBench/4DBInfer benchmark.
- **Zero-shot relational transfer.** Take the single-table curriculum model and
  evaluate it on the relational benchmark *without any relational adaptation*.

## Key results

- **Curriculum >> all-at-once (single-table).** The progressive 7→17-column
  curriculum reaches **0.703 avg ROC-AUC** on the 23-task tabular benchmark using
  only ~13,300 synthetic tables (~45× fewer single-table datasets than RDB-PFN's
  warm-up recipe). The *same data trained all-at-once collapses to 0.541* — order
  matters more than content here.
- **Massive relational data efficiency.** A relational curriculum trained from
  scratch on only ~5,500 PluRel databases hits **0.638 avg ROC-AUC** on the
  19-task RelBench/4DBInfer suite, recovering **88%** of RDB-PFN's reported
  performance with **~220× less** relational synthetic data.
- **Single-table pretraining already transfers.** The single-table curriculum
  model, evaluated directly on the relational benchmark with no relational
  adaptation, reaches **0.631** — nearly matching the dedicated relational
  pipeline (0.638).

## Fit with the tabular curriculum

Directly probes *what drives the synthetic prior* behind the Y5 RDB-PFN
relational foundation model: curriculum design and schema diversity appear to
dominate both the choice of relational generator and raw synthetic scale. The
single-table→relational transfer result is a sharp, teachable claim on the
**tabular↔relational seam** — much of "relational" competence may be latent in
well-sequenced single-table pretraining. Pairs naturally with its companion
study (PluRel-to-RDB-PFN, 2607.29129) for the Y5 Q2 pretraining-pipeline lectures.

## Caveats / limitations

- **Single generator (PluRel), single target pipeline (RDB-PFN).** Whether the
  curriculum effect generalizes to other priors/architectures is untested.
- **Recovers 88%, not 100%.** Data efficiency is bought at a real accuracy gap
  vs. the full-scale recipe; "88% at 220× less data" is a Pareto point, not a win.
- **Reported baselines.** Comparisons are against RDB-PFN's *reported* numbers
  and recipes rather than a from-scratch re-run under identical conditions.
- **Benchmark-bound.** Results rest on RelBench/4DBInfer and a 23-task tabular
  suite; the "widen 7→17 columns" schedule is one hand-designed curriculum, not a
  learned or ablated schedule space.

## Take-home

For relational PFN pretraining, *how* you sequence synthetic data beats *how
much* you have and *which* generator you use: a progressive single-table
curriculum recovers ~88% of RDB-PFN at ~220× less relational data and transfers
to the relational benchmark almost for free — while the identical data shuffled
all-at-once collapses.
