# Review — Scaling Laws for Classical Machine Learning on Tabular Data: An Empirical Benchmark Study

- **arXiv:** [2607.21866](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21866) (v2, cs.LG, Jul 2026)
- **Author:** Kaihua Ding (University of Pennsylvania / Wharton AI & Analytics)

## Problem

Neural scaling laws (Kaplan, Chinchilla, Rosenfeld) are now central to how large
models are budgeted, but the overwhelming majority of applied ML is
gradient-boosted trees, random forests and linear models on tabular business
data. A classical learning-curve literature exists but only at small scale (one
curve, one team, a handful of cells). The paper asks, **at cross-replicator
scale, whether classical tabular error follows a clean power law, whether a
single scaling exponent is shared across a model family, and how far a reported
exponent can be trusted** across nominally compliant re-implementations.

## Method

- **Distributed benchmark.** A graduate cohort of **127 independent
  replicators**, each assigned 3 of **18** public tabular datasets (10
  classification / 8 regression, 303–53,940 rows), ran a fixed protocol over
  **6 model families** (Boosting, RandomForest, SVM-RBF, Linear/Logistic, Ridge,
  Lasso), fixed default hyperparameters, single 80/20 split (seed 42), and 7
  nested training fractions {0.01…1.0}. Total: **11,536 runs, 1,648 fitted
  curves**.
- **Power-law fit.** Per (replicator, dataset, model) triple, fit the
  additive-irreducible form `error(N) = a·N^(−b) + c` (Rosenfeld
  parameterization) by non-linear least squares; metrics are 1−AUROC
  (classification) and RMSE/std(y) (regression).
- **Shared-exponent test.** Per family, fit one `b_model` shared across datasets
  with per-dataset (a_d, c_d) free, vs. an unconstrained per-dataset fit,
  compared by R² gap and AIC, plus a **random-effects meta-analysis**
  (DerSimonian–Laird) and a **data-collapse** check.
- **Stress tests.** Leave-one-dataset-out (LODO) transfer, bound sensitivity, and
  a **permutation null** (B=500) on the in-sample R² gap.
- **Reproducibility lens.** With the seed fixed, cross-replicator **CV(b) = σ_b/μ_b**
  measures protocol-drift (preprocessing/encoding/missing-value) spread, not seed
  variance.

## Key results

- **Power laws fit.** R² > 0.8 on **77.7%** of cells (74.5% at R² > 0.9). Fit
  quality is very uneven: tree ensembles / SVM / linear ≈ 88–95% good, but
  **Lasso (23.8%) and Ridge (47.5%) fit poorly** — Lasso shrinks to
  predict-the-mean (a clean negative control).
- **Approximate shared exponents.** For **5 of 6** families a single family-level
  exponent loses ≤0.011 R² vs. per-dataset fits, but AIC strongly favors the
  unconstrained fit (ΔAIC +65…+908) and data-collapse is partial (32–58% of
  points within ±0.5 dex). Framed as **approximate predictive compressibility,
  not universality**. LODO transfers cleanly for Boosting/RandomForest/Linear
  (held-out R² gap 0.007–0.035, b stable CV 0.04–0.09); SVM mild; Ridge fragile
  (LODO CV 0.96); the permutation null (p ∈ [0.55, 0.97]) shows the in-sample gap
  lacks specificity — the real signal is in the tight shared-b CIs and LODO.
- **Exponents.** Median b ≈ 0.495 overall; RandomForest ≈ 0.33, Boosting ≈ 0.48,
  Linear (classification) ≈ 0.83. These classical exponents are **larger** than
  small-NN exponents (Kaplan b≈0.095), but the **irreducible floor c** (tree
  ensembles c ≈ 0.10–0.21) is the binding constraint — "in tabular ML the floor,
  not the exponent."
- **Replicator variance.** With the seed fixed, mean **CV(b) = 0.144** across 58
  non-degenerate cells (13 cells byte-identical). SVM worst (~0.25). Practical
  rule: single-group scaling claims should carry a ~14% uncertainty floor on b,
  and comparative "A has larger b than B" claims need a ≳0.15·b gap.
- **Ranking / data requirements.** Boosting wins 50% of datasets, RandomForest
  33% (tree ensembles together 83%), reproducing "boosted trees rule tabular." A
  released N* table gives data needed to hit 1−AUROC=0.15 (e.g. ~200–500 rows on
  adult_income/bank_marketing/online_shoppers; ~16.5k for telco_churn).

## Fit with the tabular curriculum

Squarely in the **Y1 evaluation-rigor / honest-baseline thread** (TabReD →
TabArena → BeyondArena). It contributes (a) reusable **tabular learning curves +
a data-requirement table** as a benchmark resource, and (b) a first-of-its-kind
**protocol-drift reproducibility number** (CV(b) ≈ 0.14) that operationalizes
"how much should you trust a single team's reported scaling exponent." The
tree-ensembles-dominate-at-full-data result is a clean, broadened replication of
Grinsztajn et al. The floor-vs-exponent framing is a useful lecture contrast
against neural scaling laws.

## Caveats / limitations

- **Fixed default hyperparameters, no per-N tuning** → b values are lower bounds;
  Lasso/Ridge most fragile under this constraint.
- **Single split seed (42)** → CV(b) is implementation spread only; a multi-seed
  study would report larger total variance.
- **Partial blocking:** each replicator saw only 3/18 datasets, so per-cell CV is
  over different sub-cohorts; ~14% of cells (CV≥0.30) likely reflect
  implementation bugs folded into the "phenomenon."
- **Scope:** 18 small–medium clean datasets (≤53,940 rows), SVM capped at 10k
  rows; says nothing about million-row settings, and no deep-learning / TFM
  families included.
- **Shared-exponent claim is descriptive**, not a universality proof (author is
  explicit: agreement ≠ correctness).

## Take-home

A "classroom-scale" (127-replicator) benchmark showing `a·N^(−b)+c` describes
classical tabular error well, that family-level exponents are approximately (not
universally) shared, that tree ensembles dominate at full data with the
irreducible floor as the real constraint, and — most useful for the curriculum —
that a fixed-seed re-implementation still moves the fitted exponent by ~14%,
giving a concrete honesty prior for any single-group scaling claim.
