# Curriculum staging — candidate papers

Candidate papers surfaced by the quarterly currency sweep, awaiting review before promotion into [CURRICULUM.md](./CURRICULUM.md). Newest at the bottom. Each entry must pass at least one add-only-if test (new SOTA · exposes a failure mode · baseline you'll be measured against) and not be application-only or an incremental variant. Dedupe by arXiv id and title before appending.

- [A Fair Benchmarking of Deep Relational Database Learning Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03659) — Akhter, Ajendla & Samad, 2026. First systematic, protocol-consistent benchmark of deep RDB learners across five databases; finds the Relational Transformer strongest and shows deep RDB methods beat TabPFN-2.5 even on single-table tasks, with diminishing returns past hop=1–2. Directly extends the fair-comparison/honest-baseline thread (TabReD → TabArena → BeyondArena) and is a baseline suite the thesis will be measured against. (staged 2026-07-15)
- [Context-Constrained Transfer Learning for Tabular Foundation Models via Data Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04809) — Lin & Li, 2026. TL-ANDI tackles two concrete TFM failure modes — strict context-size limits and negative transfer under source/target distribution shift — via optimal-transport anchor selection plus distilled labels and residual calibration. Sharpens the Y2 Q3 "where in-context learning breaks" story with a principled transfer-learning fix. (staged 2026-07-15)
- [TabPack: Efficient Hyperparameter Ensembles for Tabular Deep Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05380) — Gorishniy, Kotelnikov, Rubachev & Babenko, 2026. From the TabM/FT-Transformer group: a single-run MLP ensemble that samples heterogeneous hyperparameters and selects members on the fly, matching extensively tuned prior methods out of the box (competitive on a laptop). A strong-default trained-DL baseline in the RealMLP/TabM line that any honest comparison must include. (staged 2026-07-15)
- [Surprisingly Simple and Effective Multi-Domain Graph Foundation Model through Graph-to-Table Alignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11374) — Hu et al., 2026. GTAlign is a text-free graph foundation model that reuses tabular foundation models by learning a graph-to-table alignment (unified graph encoder + community-guided continual pre-training + in-context inference), beating SOTA on node and graph classification. A novel, direct bridge from TFMs to structural/graph learning — the exact tabular↔relational seam the curriculum's thesis lives on. (staged 2026-07-15)
- [Towards Evaluating Data Priors for Tabular Foundation Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29241) — Türkmen, Kaya, Pfefferle & Hutter, 2026. Builds a unified interface to compare the data-generating priors of TFMs (TabPFN-style synthetic priors vs. real-data priors) independently of architecture and training protocol, finding priors drive markedly different downstream behavior. Directly deepens the Y2 Q3 "what the synthetic SCM prior encodes" lecture with a reproducible, prior-isolating methodology (open code). (staged 2026-07-15)
- [FlexTab: A Flexible Encoder-Decoder Architecture for In-Context Learning Across Diverse Tabular Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30336) — Polewczyk et al., 2026. Pairs a single task-agnostic encoder producing target-agnostic row embeddings with task-specific decoders, hitting SOTA on classification, regression, anomaly detection and entity matching while extending tabular ICL to relational entity classification (open checkpoints). A concrete architectural bridge from single-table ICL toward the relational setting the thesis targets. (staged 2026-07-15)
- [Exploring Differences Between Tabular Enterprise Data and Public Benchmarks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30452) — Kim, Schambach, Essenberger, Sres & Höhne, 2026. Shows enterprise tables differ statistically from public benchmarks and that a model strong on standard benchmarks (TabPFN, TabICL, ConTextTab) can fail on real enterprise data and vice versa. Reinforces the honest-baseline / distribution-realism thread (TabReD → TabArena → BeyondArena) and the gap between leaderboard wins and deployment. (staged 2026-07-15)
- [Probing Memorization of Tabular In-Context Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31208) — Capano & Böhler, 2026. Introduces ICLMEM, a probing framework separating context-based prediction from parametric memorization in large tabular ICL models, detecting moderate memorization (and membership-inference risk) that concentrates in low-cardinality/binary tasks but largely vanishes under realistic training. A crisp failure-mode/privacy lens for the "where TabPFN v2 breaks" critical lectures. (staged 2026-07-15)
- [Parameter-Free Encoders Remain Viable for RDB Foundation Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05476) — Xu & Wipf, 2026. Analyzes RDB foundation-model encoders when labels are provided as inputs, proving limitations on the benefit of trainable encoder parameters and empirically showing far simpler parameter-free subgraph encoders (plus a single-table FM) match strong baselines. Sharpens the Y4/Y5 encoder debate (Universal Row Encoder vs. training-free relational ICL like RDBLearn) with theory and reproducible evidence. (staged 2026-07-15)
- [TabPATE: Differentially Private Tabular In-Context Learning Without Public Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31474) — Wahdany, Jagielski, Cresswell, Dziedzic & Boenisch, 2026. Shows even basic membership-inference attacks succeed against tabular ICL, then gives a PATE-style DP defense that needs no public in-distribution data and drives attack success to near-random while keeping utility. A concrete privacy failure-mode plus mitigation that extends the "where TabPFN/ICL leaks" thread beyond the already-staged memorization probe. (staged 2026-07-15)
- [KnowsTFM: Knowledge-Informed Fine-Tuning of Small Tabular Foundation Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30258) — Koloski, Jiang, Pollak, Škrlj, Jamnik & Simidjievski, 2026. Adapts nanoscale TabPFN/TabICL-style models to niche, scarce, shifted domains via knowledge-graph structural attention priors plus low-rank updates, and documents a sharp failure mode — continual fine-tuning of frontier models can collapse pretrained knowledge and mechanisms. Grounds the Y2 Q3 story on when TFM priors help vs. break and how to steer small specialist models. (staged 2026-07-15)
- [Can Tabular In-Context Learners Generalize to Biomolecular Property Prediction?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31126) — Guan et al., 2026. Tests whether the synthetic random-causal-graph prior of TabPFN/TabICL transfers out of domain, finding that — paired with expressive pretrained encoders (ESM, ECFP/RDKit) — tabular ICL matches or beats task-specific SOTA on ProteinGym and is competitive on ADMET/MoleculeNet few-shot tasks, but only with strong representations. A reproducible probe of what the tabular ICL inductive bias actually encodes and where its transfer hinges, sharpening the Y2 Q3 prior/representation lectures. (staged 2026-07-15)
- [Structural Adversarial Attacks on Relational Deep Learning under Integrity Constraints](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07089) — Gany, Cautis & Maniu, 2026. [RDL] First adversarial-robustness study of the standard RDL pipeline: a white-box attacker rewires foreign-key references while preserving schema integrity constraints (FK validity, degree-one FK, functional dependencies), and gradient-guided edge-mask attacks degrade RelBench rel-f1 regression more than classification. Exposes a concrete RDL failure mode — the brittleness of the PK-FK heterogeneous-temporal-graph + GNN encoding — directly on RelBench, feeding the Y4 error-analysis/robustness thread. (staged 2026-07-26)
- [Tabular Foundation Models for Discrete Choice Estimation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13314) — Liu & Zhang, 2026. [tabular] Shows TFMs fail out of the box on discrete choice because their row-independence assumption clashes with set-valued choices and persistent consumer heterogeneity, then a reformulation encoding choice-set dependence + individual-level heterogeneity beats hierarchical Bayesian estimation on holdout log-likelihood and hit rate while running 16x faster. A crisp structural failure mode of the ICL row-independence prior plus a strong baseline for the honest-comparison thread. (staged 2026-07-26)
- [Topological Signatures of Context-Level Reliability in TabPFN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17962) — Hu & Ghelichi, 2026. [tabular] Uses zigzag persistent homology on TabPFN's layer representations to show that internal geometry (H0 fragmentation, H1 loop activity) is strongly associated with dataset-level reliability and correlates with Bayes error, mean absolute residual, and overconfidence across controlled synthetic topologies. A mechanistic, context-level diagnostic of when TabPFN operates in "topologically stressed" regimes — a fresh failure-mode lens for the Y2 Q3 "where TabPFN v2 breaks" lectures. (staged 2026-07-26)
- [Scaling Laws for Classical Machine Learning on Tabular Data: A Benchmark Study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21866) — Ding, 2026. [tabular] A classroom-scale distributed replication (11,536 runs, 1,648 power-law fits over 18 datasets and 6 model families) confirms tree ensembles dominate at full data and that scaling exponents are approximately shared within a model family, but exposes a reproducibility failure mode: independent re-implementations of an identical fixed-seed protocol still differ by mean CV(b)=0.144 on the fitted exponent from unconstrained preprocessing/encoding choices. Adds reusable tabular learning curves and a data-requirement table to the Y1 evaluation-rigor / honest-baseline thread. (staged 2026-08-03)
- [Empirical Evaluation of Out-Of-Distribution Performance of Tabular Foundation Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26000) — Loza et al., 2026. [tabular] Benchmarks nine TFMs (TabPFNv2/2.5/2.6/v3, TabICL, TabICLv2, Mitra, LimiX, TabFM) on TableShift label/socioeconomic/geographic shifts and finds all degrade systematically under distribution shift regardless of pretraining strategy, with a scalability gap where the strongest models exceed standard deployment memory. A fresh multi-model OOD baseline that sharpens the Y2 Q3 "where TabPFN v2 breaks" and BeyondArena non-IID threads. (staged 2026-08-03)
- [Understanding Context Sampling in TabPFN on Small Tabular Datasets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26628) — Abdullah, 2026. [tabular] Shows TabPFN accuracy and stability on small data hinge on context diversity and feature-space coverage rather than reproducing the training distribution — matching feature means alone can cost up to 0.5 AUC — while expensive K-Means / farthest-point selection matches random sampling at 2–3 orders of magnitude more cost. A crisp, reproducible diagnostic of how the in-context prior actually uses its context, for the Y2 Q3 TabPFN lectures. (staged 2026-08-03)
- [Curriculum Matters: Data-Efficient Relational PFN Pretraining with Synthetic Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29120) — Abolhasani & Ganapathy, 2026. [RDL] Using the PluRel generator, shows a progressive single-table curriculum recovers ~88% of RDB-PFN's RelBench/4DBInfer performance with ~220x less relational synthetic data (while all-at-once training collapses), and that a single-table-only curriculum transfers to the relational benchmark almost as well as a dedicated relational pipeline. Directly probes what drives the synthetic prior behind the Y5 RDB-PFN relational foundation model. (staged 2026-08-03)
- [PluRel-to-RDB-PFN: Schema-Guided Synthetic Relational Pretraining](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29129) — Abolhasani & Ganapathy, 2026. [RDL] Companion study decoupling synthetic data generation from the RDB-PFN training pipeline: an external PluRel→RDB-PFN conversion recovers ~87.6% of published RDB-PFN performance with ~55x fewer tasks and no single-table warm-up, and finds that exposing a real-world schema early ("schema-guided first") substantially beats late-stage schema adaptation. Feeds the Y5 Q2 relational pre-training-pipeline design lectures. (staged 2026-08-03)
